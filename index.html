Our Mission

A healthier nation begins with the truth.

For the first time in history, American children may live shorter, sicker lives than their parents. We spend more on health care than any nation — yet we are sicker, heavier, and more medicated than ever before.

“We will end the chronic disease epidemic by getting to the root causes — the food we eat, the chemicals around us, and the corruption that lets it continue.” — The Make America Healthy Again movement

MAHA is a non-partisan call to restore health as a national priority: real food, clean water and air, transparent science, and freedom from conflicts of interest in the agencies meant to protect us.

The Four Pillars

What the movement stands for

A simple framework for a healthier country — focused on prevention, transparency, and the root causes of disease rather than just treating symptoms.

🥦

Real Food First

Rebuild the food supply around whole, nutritious food. Remove harmful additives and dyes, reform subsidies that favor ultra-processed products, and make honest nutrition the foundation of public health.

💧

Clean Environment

Protect the air we breathe and the water we drink. Reduce exposure to toxic chemicals, microplastics, and pollutants linked to rising rates of chronic and developmental illness.

🔬

Honest Science

Demand gold-standard, transparent research free from industry capture. End conflicts of interest and publish the data so families and doctors can make truly informed decisions.

🛡️

Accountable Agencies

Restore trust in the institutions that guard public health. Break the revolving door between regulators and the industries they oversee, and put the well-being of citizens first.

🏃

Prevention Over Pills

Shift the system from managing disease to preventing it — investing in nutrition, movement, sleep, and metabolic health instead of a lifetime of prescriptions.

👨‍👩‍👧

Healthy Children

Reverse the childhood chronic disease epidemic — obesity, diabetes, allergies, and more — so the next generation can grow up healthier than the last.

Policy Priorities

From principles to action

Concrete commitments the movement champions to turn a healthier vision into reality.

Commission rigorous, independent research into why rates of obesity, diabetes, autoimmune conditions, allergies, and neurodevelopmental disorders have climbed so sharply in American children — and act on the findings.

Re-examine food additives, synthetic dyes, and preservatives — many already restricted abroad — and close loopholes that let ingredients reach store shelves without independent safety review.

Update federal dietary guidance to reflect the best available science and steer public food programs toward whole, nutrient-dense foods rather than ultra-processed products.

Increase transparency and limit the financial entanglements between regulators, researchers, and the industries they are charged with overseeing — so public health decisions serve the public.

Prioritize the cleanup of contaminated water systems and reduce community exposure to pollutants and toxic chemicals linked to chronic and developmental health problems.

Real Food Guide

Whole foods that fuel health

The foundation of a healthier America is choosing real, nutrient-dense foods. Here are some of the best high-fiber, low-calorie options you can start eating today.

🫐 Berries & Fruits

  • Raspberries 8g fiber, 64 cal
  • Blackberries 8g fiber, 62 cal
  • Strawberries 3g fiber, 49 cal
  • Blueberries 4g fiber, 85 cal
  • Apple (medium) 4g fiber, 95 cal
  • Pear (medium) 6g fiber, 101 cal

🥦 Vegetables

  • Broccoli 2.4g fiber, 31 cal
  • Cauliflower 2.3g fiber, 25 cal
  • Spinach 0.7g fiber, 7 cal
  • Bell peppers 1.9g fiber, 30 cal
  • Carrots 3.5g fiber, 52 cal
  • Brussels sprouts 3.3g fiber, 38 cal

🌱 Legumes & Proteins

  • Lentils (cooked) 15.6g fiber, 230 cal
  • Black beans (cooked) 15g fiber, 227 cal
  • Split peas (cooked) 16.3g fiber, 231 cal
  • Chickpeas (cooked) 12.5g fiber, 269 cal
  • Green beans (cooked) 4g fiber, 44 cal

🌾 Whole Grains & Seeds

  • Chia seeds (1 oz) 10g fiber, 138 cal
  • Wheat bran (1/4 cup) 6g fiber, 63 cal
  • Oat bran (1/4 cup) 7g fiber, 150 cal
  • Barley (cooked) 6g fiber, 193 cal
  • Whole wheat bread 2-3g fiber, 100-120 cal

Quick Tips to Maximize Benefits

  • ✓ Eat skins — keep apple and pear skins for extra fiber
  • ✓ Eat raw when possible — cooking can reduce fiber content
  • ✓ Mix & match — combine berries with seeds for maximum nutrition
  • ✓ Increase gradually — add fiber slowly to avoid digestive discomfort
  • ✓ Drink water — fiber works best with proper hydration (2-3L daily)
